How to work out how many curtain panels you need

Curtains look skimpy when ordered to match bare window width. Measuring the full rod or track and applying the right fullness multiplier ensures the curtains drape properly and close without gaps.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Measure the total width of the curtain pole (between finials) or track—not the window glass alone. Standard rods extend 6 to 12 in (15 to 30 cm) past each side of the frame.
  2. Pick a fullness multiplier for your heading style: 1.5× to 2× for tailored grommet or eyelet headings, and 2× to 2.5× for rod-pocket, pencil-pleat, or pinch-pleat drapes.
  3. Read the flat width of a single finished panel on the package (common US ready-made panels are 50 or 52 in wide; UK panels are often 46, 66, or 90 in).
  4. Keep the even-pairs option checked for center-opening windows so both sides hang symmetrically.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Track or rod width: Full width between finials (usually 12 to 24 in wider than window glass).
  • Desired fullness multiplier: Typically 1.5× to 2.5× depending on heading style.
  • Flat width of one panel: Single finished panel flat width (commonly 50 in US, 140 cm EU).
  • Buy an even number of panels (pairs): Recommended for standard center-opening windows.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Measuring only the glass width, which leaves light leaks and blocks daylight when curtains are pulled open.
  • Assuming "1× fullness" covers a window neatly. Flat panels stretch tight like a sheet and rarely meet cleanly in the center.
  • Forgetting return and overlap width on wraparound blackout rods or heavy lined drapes.
  • Mixing up pair width with single-panel width when reading online retail product listings.

Practical ordering checklist

  • Confirm whether the retailer sells curtains as a single panel or a pair before adding to cart.
  • Check actual fullness after rounding up to whole panels—sometimes 3 panels at 2.3× looks better than 2 panels stretched to 1.5×.
  • Measure drop length separately from rod ring or track glider to floor or sill.
